Estimating first-floor heights of houses from Street View imagery

ResultPredicted the first-floor elevation of coastal houses to within a mean absolute error of 0.28 m (Florida) and 0.31 m (Virginia) against elevation certificates, by developing object-detection models on Google Street View imagery, work published in a peer-reviewed journal.

Project
Undergraduate research (2023 to 2024) with the UF Engineering School of Sustainable Infrastructure and Environment on uncertainty quantification of machine learning for damage prediction. Published as Klepac, S., Maldonado, E., Subgranon, A., and Olabarrieta, M. (2026), “Estimating First-Floor Heights of Residential Houses from Google Street View Imagery Using Object Detection,” Journal of Computing in Civil Engineering, 40(4).
Problem
Flood damage models need the height of each building's first floor above ground, but that figure normally comes from elevation certificates that most houses do not have and that are costly to survey.
Solution
Collected, cleaned and annotated large image datasets, trained and validated region-based object detectors (Cascade R-CNN and Faster R-CNN in Detectron2), and compared predicted heights with surveyed certificates in two states. Contributed to conceptualization, data curation, formal analysis, investigation, methodology and manuscript review.

CES 6165 · Concrete Structural Rehabilitation / Spring 2026 / Individual report

Champlain Towers South: Shear Collar Repair Design

ResultCut the punching-shear demand-to-capacity ratio at the critical pool-deck column from 1.74 to 0.81, a 115% gain in design capacity (108.9 to 234.4 kip), by designing a 40-inch circularly reinforced concrete shear collar and an ACI 562-21 rehabilitation plan.

Project
A 43-page forensic and repair study of the 2021 partial collapse of the 12-story Champlain Towers South condominium in Surfside, Florida, built on the published NIST and WJE investigations.
Problem
The flat-plate pool deck had no shear reinforcement and was under-designed for punching shear from the start (as-designed ratio 1.38). Top bars placed with 2 in. of cover instead of the specified 3/4 in. pushed the as-built ratio to 1.74, and visible warning signs were not acted on.
Solution
Recalculated punching shear with unbalanced moment transfer for the as-designed and as-built conditions, then sized a collar with three No. 5 hoops and twelve No. 3 ties using the Klein and Gouwens method. Wrote a six-step installation sequence that works from the garage below, and mapped the repair to the assessment, design, durability and QA chapters of ACI 562-21.
  • ACI 318-25
  • ACI 562-21
  • Punching shear
  • Repair detailing

CEG 6117 · Deep Foundations / Project manager · Team of 5

SR-20 Apalachicola River Bridge: Pier 58 Foundation Alternatives

ResultCompared two deep-foundation designs for a river bridge pier and recommended six 72-inch drilled shafts (demand/capacity 0.39) over 24 battered driven piles (0.955) despite a $251k higher estimate, by managing a five-person team and modeling the driven-pile option in FB-MultiPier.

Project
Analysis and design of the foundation for Pier 58 of the SR-20 bridge between Blountstown and Bristol, Florida, to FDOT and AASHTO requirements.
Problem
The pier sits in the river on dense sand over limestone. The foundation had to carry the pier loads within displacement limits, be drivable with hammer energy capped at 150 kip-ft because of a neighboring bridge, and make sense on cost and constructability.
Solution
The team built the soil profile, got axial capacities from FB-Deep, checked drivability in GRLWEAP (APE 15-4 hammer, 36 to 88 blows per foot, stresses within FDOT 455), modeled both pile groups in FB-MultiPier, designed the caps, and priced each option with FDOT bridge cost data ($1.73M driven piles, $1.99M drilled shafts). Shafts were recommended for capacity margin, scour resistance and a smaller cofferdam.
  • FB-MultiPier
  • FB-Deep
  • GRLWEAP

Seismic Member Selection for a Steel Warehouse

ResultSelected HP12X84 columns with HP10X57 beams for a Northern California warehouse, holding member stress under 80% of yield (28.8 ksi) in both directions, by building a spreadsheet that screened 22 HP sections against the ASCE 7-22 multi-period design spectrum.

Project
Seismic design and member selection for a Risk Category II data-storage warehouse on stiff soil, framed with A36 steel HP shapes and fixed beam-column connections.
Problem
Pick the most economical column and beam sections that keep stresses acceptable under earthquake loading in both the short and long directions, when the load itself depends on the stiffness and period of the sections chosen.

CGN 6905 · Scour and Erosion in Coastal Environments / Spring 2026 / Individual Project

Do Scour Equations Hold Up at Real Offshore Wind Farms?

ResultShowed that five standard scour equations all give a negative R² against field measurements at 311 offshore wind monopiles, and that the DNV equation predicts zero scour at every one, by running each equation on a public dataset from six British wind farms and flagging every prediction made outside its validated range.

Project
A comparison of five empirical scour-depth equations (Sumer and Fredsøe, DNV, Rudolph and Bos, Qi and Gao, HEC-18) against measured scour around monopile foundations on sandy seabeds.
Problem
Monopile foundations are designed with equations calibrated on small laboratory piles. It was unclear how well they predict scour at full-scale turbines, or how often they are being used outside the conditions they were validated for.
Solution
Filtered the Garcia et al. (2025) dataset to fine and medium sand sites, derived the wave and current parameters for each turbine, applied all five equations, and compared bias, RMSE, MAE and R² for in-range and out-of-range cases. Only 0 to 70% of turbines fell inside each equation's validated range. HEC-18 fit best, but only because the sites are current-dominated.

  • Empirical scour models
  • Error statistics

CES 6588 · Protective Structures / Co-author · Team of 8

Blast-Resistant Concrete Corner Joints: Closing a Gap in UFC 3-340-02

ResultCo-authored a ten-step design procedure and a draft replacement code section for corner joints in blast-resistant concrete, addressing joints that can fail at 32% of theoretical moment capacity, by reviewing four decades of research against UFC 3-340-02.

Project
A 29-page research paper on how reinforced concrete corner joints behave under blast loading and how the U.S. Department of Defense design manual treats them.
Problem
UFC 3-340-02 says special corner reinforcement should be provided but gives no quantitative rules, while tests show joint detailing decides whether a corner reaches 32% or more than 100% of its theoretical capacity.
Solution
Reviewed experimental, analytical and finite-element studies from 1976 to 2021 and pulled out the rules they agree on: diagonal bars of at least half the main tension steel, inner haunches of about 4/9 of member thickness, confinement where steel ratios exceed 0.5%, and plastic hinges relocated away from the support face. Turned these into a design procedure and draft provisions in the manual's own format.
  • UFC 3-340-02
  • Blast design

Self-Centering Steel Plate Shear Walls

ResultReviewed self-centering steel plate shear walls, which tolerate up to 4% drift (against 2% for conventional walls) with residual drift under 0.5% in tests, and proposed design-code recommendations, by synthesizing experimental and numerical studies.

Project
A state-of-the-art review of a seismic lateral system that adds post-tensioned rocking connections and replaceable web plates to the conventional steel plate shear wall.
Problem

Solution

Elevated Walkways and Pedestrian-Level Wind Comfort

ResultProposed design guidelines for elevated walkways (width ratio of 0.4 or less, overall mean velocity ratio above 0.3) to limit street-level ventilation losses of up to 35%, by synthesizing large eddy simulation and field studies of urban street canyons.

Project
A conference-style paper on how elevated pedestrian walkways change wind conditions for people on the street below in dense cities.
Problem

Solution

CES 3102 · Structural Analysis / Individual Assignment

Lateral systems compared: rigid, pinned and braced frames

ResultShowed that diagonal bracing cut the roof drift of a multi-story frame from 1.68 in. to 0.46 in. (73%) and the peak moment from 139.0 to 33.5 kip-ft compared with rigid moment connections, by modeling three lateral systems in Ftool.

Project
A structural analysis of one frame under gravity and wind load with three ways of resisting lateral load: rigid connections, shear connections only, and a braced bay.
Problem
Determine which system keeps drift between floors under the 3/8 in. limit used to protect facades and partitions, and what each one costs in member forces.
Solution
Modeled each case and compared shear, moment and deflected shape. The rigid frame exceeded the drift limit, the pinned frame drifted 19.8 in. at the roof, and the braced frame passed. Doubling the brace section reduced roof drift further, to 0.29 in.
